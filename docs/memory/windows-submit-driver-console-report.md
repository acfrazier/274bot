# Windows submit diagnostic: driver and console comparison

Date: 2026-09-07

Status: diagnostic comparison only. This report does not establish a driver-only causal attribution, a final latency result, or performance acceptance.

## Bottom line

The signed NVIDIA 616.86 hotfix was installed and confirmed after reboot as native driver version 32.0.16.1686. It did not produce an observed cadence improvement across the two RDP observations: the 616.56 cell had 16.6388422 client ticks/s and 16.51285395 GPU callbacks/s; the 616.86 cell had 16.2684974 client ticks/s and 16.16781566 GPU callbacks/s. The 616.86 local-console cell had 48.9132178 client ticks/s and 48.92992317 GPU callbacks/s, with acquire and submit timings near zero rather than roughly 30 ms in RDP.

The console result is a strong diagnostic contrast, not a narrow driver conclusion. RDP versus local console, display geometry/scale, Windows session and presentation path, and focus state changed together. The evidence therefore supports “RDP/presentation conditions are involved” more strongly than “616.86 fixes the problem,” while the architecture-level cause remains under investigation.

## Submit-trace interpretation

The three overlap summaries have these semantics:

- They cover all emitted call-expression records, including warmup, captures, and teardown. They are not steady-state-only windows.
- The old-driver RDP summary has 241 events, with 7,789 acquire records and a 30.5489 ms mean acquire duration.
- The new-driver RDP summary has 233 events, with 7,558 acquire records and a 30.5404 ms mean acquire duration.
- The console summary has 236 events, with 11,549 acquire records and a 0.0194 ms mean acquire duration.
- Long records are nearly fully associated with acquire overlap in both RDP traces. This association is not proof of a dynamic lock, lock ownership, or GPU execution cause.
- The submission expression includes evaluation of the `encoder.finish` argument. The measured span is therefore not an isolated lock measurement.
- No unemitted tail is inferred, and the disabled diagnostic path remains uninstrumented.

The console difference is therefore real within this diagnostic, but it cannot by itself identify whether the limiting mechanism is the RDP display/session path, a presentation/fence interaction, scheduling, a driver behavior, or an interaction among those components.

## Native platform and desktop conditions

The old and new RDP cells both started at 2240x1160 with scale 2. The local-console cell started at 1680x870 with scale 1.5. The same NVIDIA GPU and Vulkan/Fifo configuration were selected in the submit traces, but GPU callback delivery is CPU-side completion after a prior submit; it is not a hardware GPU timestamp or physical scanout measurement.

The driver installation receipt records a signed setup executable and exact device match for the 616.86 hotfix. The installer required a reboot; the reboot completed, and native preflight confirmed NVIDIA `32.0.16.1686`. The prior driver backup remains at:

`C:\ProgramData\274bot-Test\driver-backup-61656-20260907`

The Hyper-V builder was Off for the relevant preflights. Dell/Office quiet services were stopped as declared, and Intel graphics was preserved. These controls reduce known host noise but do not remove the display/session confounder.

Each desktop observation was a separate read-only observation, not part of the managed sampler role map. The observer was briefly foreground with PowerShell. Startup focus was cached; there is no proof that the application remained focused for the whole run. The RDP cells were remote session 2; the console cell was local. The capture-time console PNGs show:

1. scene-ready guard-thieving state with four food;
2. bank view with 22 food and the closing-bank overlay;
3. return-route state with a 22-food overlay.

These captures preserve functional scene evidence only. They do not prove whole-run focus, cadence, physical-display presentation, or scanout.

## Bounded causal interpretation

The evidence is consistent with the following bounded statement:

> In the frozen diagnostic build, switching from RDP to the local console under 616.86 changed the observed submit/acquire behavior from approximately 30.5 ms acquires and approximately 16 client ticks/s to approximately 0.019 ms acquires and approximately 49 client ticks/s. Replacing 616.56 with 616.86 across these RDP observations did not improve that behavior. Because presentation geometry, session type, presentation path, focus conditions, and server/restart/slot identity were not held identical across all cells, the experiment does not isolate a driver-only cause.

This is the approved sections 4B/5 attribution level: preserve the original failure, identify the measured contrast, and keep causal alternatives explicit. It is not a reason to redesign scheduling/rendering solely from a mean timing change.
